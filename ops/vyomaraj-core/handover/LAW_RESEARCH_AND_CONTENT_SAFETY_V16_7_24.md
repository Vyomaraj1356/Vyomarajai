# Law & Content Safety Research — V16.7.24 / Build 194

**Research snapshot:** 1 October 2026
**Coverage requested:** entertainment, social platforms, publishing, monetization, IT/cyber law, child safety, privacy, copyright, and sensitive/religious content, with Indian and international context.

## 1. Important boundaries

Requirements depend on the actual operator, hosting/intermediary and publisher roles, content formats, audience (including whether children are targeted), data flows, monetization, and countries/states where the service is established or offered. Have qualified counsel map those facts to current law before launch. This report deliberately distinguishes enacted law from regulator guidance and draft proposals. Re-check official sources and commencement notices before relying on dates.

## 2. India — national law and operational implications

| Area | Official material to assess | Handover action / open question |
|---|---|---|
| Social platforms, UGC and digital publishing | Information Technology Act, 2000; MeitY's consolidated IT (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules, 2021, updated 10 February 2026. The Rules address intermediary due diligence, grievance handling and publisher obligations; the applicable Part and duties depend on the service's actual role. The 2026 text includes provisions concerning synthetically generated information (SGI). | Classify each surface as an intermediary, publisher, online-curated-content service, or another role with Indian counsel. Maintain a complaint/takedown and appeal route, an accountable grievance owner, content records, and human review. Determine whether SGI labels/declarations apply to each product and distribution path. Do not assume a social account or embedded third-party service automatically gives the project a particular safe-harbour status. |
| Privacy and personal data | Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDP Act), commencement notification G.S.R. 843(E), DPDP Rules, 2025 G.S.R. 846(E), and corrigendum G.S.R. 892(E). The Act and Rules have phased commencement. As published, the Act's main processing duties in sections 3–17 and corresponding core Rules 3, 5–16, 22–23 are scheduled 18 months after 13 November 2025 (13 May 2027 by calendar calculation); Rule 4 and specified Act provisions have a one-year phase (13 November 2026 by calculation). Rules 1–2 and 17–21 commenced on publication. The 11 December 2025 Gazette corrigendum corrects wording in the Rules; it does not change the stated phase dates. | These dates are calculated from official notifications, not a claim that every duty is currently in force. Verify subsequent Gazette changes and counsel's interpretation. Build a data inventory; purpose/notice and consent flows; access, correction, erasure and grievance processes; processor contracts; retention limits; breach response; child-data and parental-consent controls; and transfer assessment before the relevant duties commence. Identify what data the AI, WhatsApp, hosting and analytics providers receive. |
| Child safety and prohibited sexual content | Protection of Children from Sexual Offences Act, 2012 (including reporting provisions); IT Act sections 67, 67A and 67B; IT Rules. Applicability, reporting duties and handling steps require specialist review. | No creation, generation, upload, hosting, transformation, linking, monetization or redistribution of child sexual abuse/exploitation material. Immediately restrict access and escalate a credible case to the designated child-safety/legal lead for lawful reporting and evidence instructions; do not copy or forward the media. Assess age ratings, age gates, child-directed design, parental controls, live features and safeguarding before launch. |
| Religion, identity, defamation and public-order-sensitive content | Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, including sections 196 (promoting enmity), 299 (deliberate and malicious acts intended to outrage religious feelings), 302 (uttering words with deliberate intent to wound religious feelings) and 356 (defamation); IT Act/Rules and other applicable law. Precise elements and defences are fact-specific. | Use contextual human review for religious, political, historical, news and identity-related content. Do not equate lawful worship, scholarship, satire or criticism with unlawful content. Check source accuracy, intent, context, identifiable-person allegations, translations and local-language nuance; escalate potentially inflammatory or defamatory material before publication. |
| Copyright, music, film, performance and personality rights | Copyright Act, 1957; applicable licence contracts and platform policies. Copyright exceptions are jurisdiction- and fact-specific. | Maintain an asset register with creator, source, licence/permission, territory, term, platform, editing/derivative and monetization rights. Obtain clearances for music, film clips, images, scripts, performances, logos, and voice/likeness. A credit line or disclaimer is not a licence. |
| Ads, sponsorships, affiliate links and consumer claims | Consumer Protection Act, 2019; CCPA's misleading-advertisement and endorsement guidelines; ASCI influencer/social-media disclosure guidance (self-regulatory guidance, not a statute). | Label paid, gifted, affiliate and sponsored content clearly; substantiate product, health, earning and performance claims; keep approvals and evidence. Check platform-specific branded-content tools and category restrictions. |
| Finance and investment monetization | SEBI's 22 October 2024 circular concerning association by SEBI-regulated persons and their agents with certain persons, plus applicable securities/advertising rules. | Do not publish personalized investment advice or promises of return. Route security recommendations, paid financial promotions, lead generation and any claim of registration to qualified compliance/legal review; verify the promoter's status and required disclosures. |
| IT/cyber incidents and security | IT Act; CERT-In Directions of 28 April 2022, including specified incident-reporting and log-retention requirements for covered entities. Exact scope depends on entity/service facts. | Create an incident-response owner and escalation contact; inventory systems, processors and logs; assess whether CERT-In directions apply and whether required reporting/retention can be met. Do not claim a reporting deadline or storage location is satisfied until scope and infrastructure are verified. |
| Film/exhibition | Cinematograph Act, 1952 and applicable certification rules for relevant public exhibition; digital streaming may raise separate IT Rules publisher questions. | Classify theatrical/public exhibition, online curated catalogue, social clip, and promotional use separately. Obtain required certification/ratings and local permissions where applicable. |

## 4. Moderation/publishing controls proposed for the project

The companion [`CONTENT_SAFETY_POLICY_V16_7_24.json`](CONTENT_SAFETY_POLICY_V16_7_24.json) is a **proposed policy specification only**. It is not a deployed classifier or a functioning moderation integration. Minimum workflow:

1. Label intended audience, content type, countries, platform and monetization before drafting/publishing.
2. Block child sexual exploitation material, non-consensual intimate imagery, credible threats, doxxing and other clearly prohibited content from publication; route credible child-safety cases to the designated specialist without making extra copies.
3. Require human review for religion/identity, child access, synthetic media, health/finance, news/current affairs, defamation, graphic violence and high-impact allegations.
4. Check privacy/consent, source accuracy, copyright/likeness rights, sponsorship disclosure, age rating and platform-specific terms.
5. Use an owner/editor release approval, complaint/takedown route, appeal/correction route, minimal access-controlled decision log, and jurisdiction-specific escalation.
6. Reassess when the audience, data flow, monetization, country, platform, or law changes.

This is a safety baseline, not a blanket censoring rule and not legal compliance. Lawful religious, political, educational, historical, critical, journalistic, satirical and fictional content should receive context-sensitive review.
