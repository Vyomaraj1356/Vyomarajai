# Vyomaraj Education & Public Knowledge Fabric v1.0

**Status:** Proposed on feature branch; requires CI, review and merge before becoming part of `main`.  
**System:** VYOMARAJ-AI-STUDIO  
**Owners:** User/Owner ultimate authority; Vyomaraj orchestrates; Jarvis coordinates execution; Hermes verifies provenance, safety and quality.  
**Languages:** English, Hindi, Hinglish; expand to Indian regional and global languages through reviewed localization.

## Mission

Build a lifelong-learning knowledge fabric for children, families, students, teachers, citizens and professionals. Teach the **history, purpose, functions, roles, responsibilities, categories, hierarchy, processes, benefits, limitations, risks and real-world examples** of each subject. Use age-appropriate cartoons, stories, diagrams, quizzes, simulations, short videos, long lessons, audio, printable activities and accessible formats.

## Common lesson schema

Every topic should have:
1. Stable topic ID, title, aliases and language.
2. Learning audience and prerequisites; age/grade band (early years, primary, middle, secondary, higher education, adult/citizen learning).
3. Learning outcomes and vocabulary.
4. Historical development and timeline, with region/culture context where relevant.
5. Definition, purpose, parts/categories and taxonomy.
6. Functions, roles, responsibilities, hierarchy/reporting relationships and workflow where applicable.
7. Benefits, limitations, risks, misconceptions and safe practices.
8. Everyday examples, case studies, activities and discussion questions.
9. Storyboard / cartoon characters / scene-by-scene narration, with factual notes.
10. Quiz, answer key, rubric and retrieval-practice prompts.
11. Accessibility: captions, transcript, alt text, readable contrast, dyslexia-friendly options and low-bandwidth versions.
12. Source list, source date, confidence, reviewer, rights/provenance, last-reviewed date and update trigger.

**Important distinctions:** A biological classification is not an administrative hierarchy; government structures must distinguish constitutional offices, elected representatives, civil services and local bodies; health content must not replace professional advice; historic practices must not be presented as current law without verification.

## Curriculum domains

### EDU-GOV — Government departments and public institutions
Cover the Union, State/UT and local levels; ministries/departments; constitutional bodies; regulators; commissions; courts; police and public safety; defence; health; education; agriculture; food and civil supplies; transport; environment and forests; water and sanitation; energy; labour; social justice; women and child development; finance/tax; statistics; disaster management; science and technology; digital/public service delivery; local government; public-sector institutions.

For each institution: origin/history, legal basis and jurisdiction, mandate, services, leadership and organizational chart, roles and responsibilities, decision-making and accountability, recruitment/career pathways where public information is available, citizen entitlements and grievance channels, budgets/procurement at an educational level, coordination with other bodies, limitations and current reforms. Explain that structures vary by jurisdiction and change over time. Distinguish Union, State/UT, district, block/tehsil and local service delivery rather than implying every department uses the same chain.

### EDU-LEGISLATURE — Parliament, State legislatures and local democracy
- **Parliament of India:** President's constitutional role; Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha; members and presiding officers; sessions; summons, agenda, Question Hour, Zero Hour, motions, debates, committees, bills, budget and grants, voting, laws, privileges, ethics, parliamentary questions and public records.
- **State level:** Governor's constitutional role, Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha), Legislative Council (Vidhan Parishad) only in states that have one, Chief Minister and Council of Ministers, Speaker/Chairperson, sessions, bills, budget, committees, questions, state-level lawmaking and oversight.
- **Local democracy:** Gram Sabha and Gram Panchayat; intermediate/block Panchayat where applicable; Zila Parishad; urban Nagar Panchayat, Municipal Council and Municipal Corporation; elected representatives, local officials, ward committees where applicable, local plans, services, budgets, social audit and citizen participation.
- Explain election, representation, lawmaking, executive accountability, federal division of powers, constitutional safeguards and how citizens can follow official proceedings. Do not imply that the same structure applies identically in every state/UT or municipality.

### EDU-FOOD — Fruits, vegetables, dry fruits and food literacy
- Fruit and vegetable families, edible plant parts, culinary vs botanical classifications, seasonality, local/regional/global varieties, growing regions, harvesting, storage, transport, food systems and history of domestication/trade.
- Dry fruits: distinguish dried fruits (e.g. raisins, dates, figs) from commonly called tree nuts and seeds (e.g. almonds, walnuts, pistachios, cashews, peanuts and seeds); clarify that names and culinary grouping differ from botanical classification.
- Nutrients and balanced diets, fibre, hydration, dietary diversity, portion awareness, food safety, washing/handling/storage, spoilage, allergies and choking hazards for young children. Avoid miracle-food claims, disease-cure claims or universal serving prescriptions; nutrition needs vary by age and health.
- Content templates: “from seed to plate”, food-origin map, plant-part detective, market math, seasonal calendar, kitchen-science demonstration and waste-reduction activity.

### EDU-HYGIENE — Hygiene, health habits and sanitation
Personal hygiene, handwashing, oral/dental care, bathing and skin care, hair/nail care, menstrual health and dignity, toilet hygiene, safe water, food hygiene, household cleaning, laundry, respiratory etiquette, waste segregation, vector prevention, school hygiene, community sanitation, public toilets and environmental health.
Teach history and development of sanitation/public health, purpose and methods, supplies, common myths, safe routines and age-appropriate independence. Explain when to seek a trusted adult or qualified health professional. No shame, stigma, body shaming or unsupported medical claims. Use public-health and health-authority sources, with review dates.

### EDU-STUDIES — All major fields of study and lifelong learning
Create a navigable taxonomy covering:
- Languages, literacy, literature, communication and linguistics.
- Mathematics, statistics, logic and computational thinking.
- Natural sciences: physics, chemistry, biology, ecology, geology, astronomy and Earth systems.
- Social sciences: history, geography, civics, economics, psychology, sociology and anthropology.
- Computing, AI, data, robotics, cybersecurity and digital/media literacy.
- Engineering, design, architecture and applied technology.
- Health, medicine literacy, nursing, nutrition, public health and sports science (educational, not diagnostic).
- Agriculture, horticulture, animal science, food systems, forestry and fisheries.
- Business, accounting, finance literacy, entrepreneurship, management and commerce.
- Law, governance, public administration, ethics and human rights.
- Arts, music, dance, theatre, film, crafts, photography and creative writing.
- Vocational/technical trades, skilled work, safety and career exploration.
- Environment, sustainability, climate, energy, oceans and disaster preparedness.
- Philosophy, comparative religion, cultures, mythology and Indian Knowledge Systems, clearly distinguishing faith/tradition, interpretation and independently verifiable historical/scientific claims.
For each field: history, questions it investigates, methods/tools, branches, key concepts, applications, career paths, ethics, limitations, landmark discoveries and age-appropriate hands-on activities.

### EDU-EARTH-SPACE — Earth, oceans, weather and universe
Earth's formation and layers; rocks, minerals, plate tectonics, landforms, soil, water cycle, atmosphere, weather vs climate, ecosystems, biodiversity, seasons, day/night, Moon phases, eclipses, Solar System, Sun and stars, galaxies, cosmic scale, space exploration, satellites and their uses, observation tools, scientific models, evidence and uncertainty. Teach safe sky observation and never look directly at the Sun without certified protection. Include climate and disaster literacy without sensationalism.

### EDU-SOCIETY — People, communities and citizenship
Family and community systems (without assuming one family structure), culture and language, social institutions, cooperation, empathy, communication, conflict resolution, inclusion, disability awareness, gender respect, child rights, elder care, civic duties, public services, volunteering, media literacy, misinformation detection, digital citizenship, privacy, consent, personal safety, community history and local occupations. Present cultures fairly and avoid stereotypes; distinguish description from endorsement.

### EDU-LIFE-SKILLS — Everyday knowledge
Money and consumer literacy, time management, study skills, critical thinking, problem-solving, first-aid awareness, road and transport safety, emergency preparedness, home and electrical safety, environmental stewardship, basic legal/citizen literacy, online safety, career awareness and responsible use of AI. Safety-critical lessons must be reviewed and must not encourage children to perform hazardous tasks without adults.

## Cartoon and multimedia production system

Use a recurring, culturally inclusive cast of original characters (for example, a curious child, a friendly guide, a scientist, a community helper and a fact-checking mascot). Never imitate copyrighted characters or use real people’s likenesses without permission.

Each episode pipeline:
**LEARNING OBJECTIVE → SOURCE RESEARCH → FACT CHECK → AGE / CULTURE / SAFETY REVIEW → SCRIPT → STORYBOARD → CARTOON / DIAGRAM / DEMO → CAPTIONS & TRANSLATION → QUIZ → PILOT TEST → PUBLISH GATE → FEEDBACK & REVISION**

Recommended formats:
- 30–90 second concept shorts (one learning outcome).
- 3–7 minute story/cartoon lessons.
- 8–20 minute structured explainers for older learners.
- Interactive diagrams, timelines, role-play, quizzes, printable worksheets and parent/teacher guides.
- Audio-only versions, sign-language/accessibility variants where available, captions, transcripts and low-data downloads.

Character-led storytelling must simplify presentation, not distort facts. Separate fictional dialogue from factual narration. Clearly label dramatizations, uncertainty and disputed interpretations.

## Proposed hierarchy: VIDYA → Education sub-agents

**VIDYA (Education Knowledge Fabric)** is the parent education agent under the Vyomaraj/Jarvis orchestration layer. The roles below are proposed **sub-agents/specialist roles inside VIDYA's education domain**, not independent top-level system agents. They must be registered only after the existing central registry schema and identifiers are checked.

- **Curriculum and learning:** PATHYA (curriculum/assessment), ITIHASA (history/timelines), VIGYANA (science/Earth/space), SHASANA (government/civics), SAMAJA (society/citizenship).
- **Food, health and daily living:** ANNA (food/nutrition literacy), SHUCHI (hygiene/sanitation literacy), CHAI (tea/beverages), SWASTHYA (yoga/fitness/daily routines).
- **Products, workplaces and industries:** UPKARAN (household tools/gadgets/stationery), KARYALAYA (office/workplace literacy), UDYOG (industry history/value chains/jobs), SHILPA (regional crafts/manufacturing), SURAKSHA (consumer/product safety; shared child-safety capability).
- **Learning media and localization:** KATHA (stories/cartoon scripts), CHITRA (visual learning), BHASHA (translation/localization), MULYANKAN (quizzes/learning analytics).
- **Trust, rights and release:** PRAMANA (fact-check/provenance), ADHIKARA (rights/licensing), PRAKASHAN (publishing/distribution, only after authorization).

**Shared platform services—not education sub-agents:** HERMES remains the cross-system verification/resilience layer; KUBER remains the cross-system finance/revenue/rights-reconciliation layer. They may support education workflows but are not children of VIDYA.

Reuse existing shared RESEARCH, WEB_RESEARCH, FACT_CHECK, CONTENT_SAFETY, CREATIVE, MEDIA, TRANSLATION, QUALITY, ANALYTICS and PUBLISH agents where available; do not duplicate agents unnecessarily. The proposed roles are design labels until validated and registered. Do not change registry counts or claim deployment merely because this specification names them.

## Sources and factual governance

Start with official primary sources where possible:
- NCERT and DIKSHA for curriculum and school learning resources.
- India Code, Constitution of India, Parliament of India, Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha official resources for constitutional and parliamentary education.
- Ministry of Panchayati Raj and state/UT government portals for local governance.
- Relevant government health, food-safety, agriculture, environment and space-science agencies for domain facts.
- Peer-reviewed literature and recognized educational/scientific institutions for broader or disputed topics.

Use official sources as starting points, not as a reason to suppress credible disagreement. Cite source URLs and access/review dates inside lesson metadata. Mark jurisdiction-specific, time-sensitive content for periodic revalidation.

## Quality gates

A lesson cannot be marked verified/published unless:
- factual claims have traceable sources and date-sensitive claims are checked;
- constitutional/legal descriptions identify jurisdiction and avoid confusing convention with law;
- health/nutrition guidance is cautious, evidence-based and reviewed;
- child safety, accessibility, cultural respect and rights/provenance checks pass;
- translation preserves meaning and terminology;
- quizzes match the stated learning outcomes;
- public release passes the configured owner/policy gate;
- corrections can be propagated to scripts, videos, captions, quizzes and translations.

**Initial status:** curriculum specification proposed; no claim that all lessons, cartoons, agents, integrations or publications already exist or are live.


### EDU-DAILY-LIFE — Tea, yoga, fitness and everyday routines
- **Tea and everyday beverages:** plant and cultural history; tea-growing regions and local traditions; types such as green, black, white, oolong and herbal infusions (clarify that “herbal tea” may contain no tea plant); leaf-to-cup processing; brewing tools; taste, caffeine, safe handling, storage, sustainability and responsible consumption. Distinguish cultural practices from medical claims and note that caffeine suitability varies by age and individual circumstances.
- **Yoga, movement and fitness:** historical and cultural context; distinguish yoga's diverse traditions and philosophical dimensions from modern exercise formats; explain mobility, balance, strength, endurance, warm-up, cool-down, rest, hydration and age-appropriate activity. Include beginner-safe, inclusive modifications and guidance to stop if pain, dizziness or unusual symptoms occur; avoid claims that yoga or exercise guarantees cures.
- **Daily routines:** sleep habits, personal organization, meal planning, hydration, breaks, posture, screen use, household routines, commuting and time management. Avoid one-size-fits-all prescriptions and shame-based messaging.
- **Learning formats:** a tea leaf journey map, a safe desk-stretch cartoon, “movement breaks at school/office,” a morning-routine planner and a cultural tea traditions series.

### EDU-HOUSEHOLD-OFFICE — Everyday-use goods, tools, gadgets and stationery
Teach items used at home, school, office, shops, workshops and companies, including:
- **Household essentials:** cookware, utensils, cleaning tools, storage, lighting, fans, appliances, furniture, plumbing basics, water filters and energy-saving devices.
- **Office and company tools:** desks, chairs, filing/storage, printers/scanners, phones, monitors, routers, meeting-room equipment, access systems and shared workplace supplies.
- **Stationery and learning tools:** paper, notebooks, pens, pencils, erasers, rulers, markers, staplers, folders, art materials, calculators, whiteboards and digital note-taking tools.
- **Gadgets and electronics:** purpose, components, operating principles at a suitable level, categories, compatibility, power/battery use, maintenance, repairability, accessibility, privacy, cybersecurity, safe disposal and e-waste.
- **For every product family:** origin and evolution; purpose and functions; users and roles; types/categories; key features; benefits and limitations; selection criteria; cost/total cost of ownership; safe use; cleaning and maintenance; troubleshooting boundaries; warranties and consumer rights; recycling and lifecycle impacts.
- **Safety rule:** never encourage children to open mains-powered devices, handle hazardous chemicals, misuse tools or bypass safety guards. Use unplugged/demo equipment and adult supervision for practical lessons. Avoid unverified product endorsements.

### EDU-INDUSTRY — Global, Indian, state, district and regional industries
Build a searchable industry atlas from primary production to end user, covering traditional, modern and emerging sectors:
- Agriculture and allied sectors; food and beverages (including tea); textiles, handloom and apparel; handicrafts; mining and minerals; metals and materials; construction and building materials; energy and utilities; water and waste; transport, logistics and mobility; manufacturing and industrial machinery; electronics and semiconductors; consumer goods and appliances; pharmaceuticals and healthcare; biotechnology; IT, software, AI and telecom; media, entertainment, gaming and publishing; education; tourism and hospitality; retail and e-commerce; banking, insurance and financial services; professional and public services; aerospace and defence; marine/fisheries; forestry and bioeconomy; renewable energy; recycling and circular economy.
- **Geography:** compare global value chains with Indian national context and state/district/cluster/local specialties. Use reliable official statistics and industry sources; record year, geography, definitions and uncertainty. Do not present changing rankings or employment/output numbers without dates.
- **Industry lifecycle:** natural/raw inputs → farming/mining/sourcing → processing → components → manufacturing/assembly → quality and safety checks → packaging → warehousing → transport → wholesale/retail/service → customer use → repair/reuse/recycling. Adapt the map to sectors that do not follow this exact sequence.
- **Industry roles and operating model:** workers and skilled trades, farmers/artisans, engineers, designers, operators, supervisors, quality and safety teams, research and development, procurement, finance, HR, sales/marketing, logistics, regulators, standards bodies, customers, communities and investors. Explain that reporting structures differ by organization and sector; do not impose one universal hierarchy.
- **History and impact:** invention and craft origins, industrial revolutions, mechanization/digitalization, local knowledge, trade and migration, entrepreneurship, working conditions, occupational safety, skills, wages (use verified data), environmental footprint, resource efficiency, labor rights, inclusion, innovation, market competition, consumer protection and resilience.
- **Business literacy:** how a product is designed, costed, made, tested, priced, marketed, sold, serviced and accounted for; simple supply-demand and quality examples; explain jobs and skills without promising employment or investment returns.
- **Local learning:** visit/virtual-tour lesson templates for farms, tea gardens, workshops, small businesses, factories, warehouses, offices, labs and recycling facilities. Do not enter hazardous or restricted workplaces; use permissioned footage and safety review.

### EDU-CONSUMER-SAFETY — Product literacy and responsible buying
Teach labels, instructions, age suitability, quality marks where applicable, electrical and battery safety, food/contact-material safety, counterfeit awareness, warranties, repair options, privacy permissions, data collection, planned obsolescence, accessibility, energy use, product recalls and end-of-life disposal. For India, use current official consumer-protection, BIS, electrical-safety and e-waste sources; explain that a mark or certification only means what its official scope says. Never imply every item needs the same certification.

### Additional cartoon / explainer formats
- “How it is made” — from raw material to finished product, including people and safety.
- “Who does what?” — role cards for an industry or workplace, with no universal hierarchy assumed.
- “Fix, reuse or recycle?” — safe decision-making for common household goods and gadgets.
- “Tea around the world / Tea in India” — regions, farming, processing, livelihoods and trade.
- “Yoga and movement for everyday life” — age-appropriate and inclusive, not a substitute for healthcare.
- “Stationery detectives” — compare tools, materials, function, cost, durability and waste.
- “One product, many jobs” — design, sourcing, manufacturing, quality, logistics, sales, repair and recycling.
- “Local industry explorer” — a verified regional case study linked to national and global context.

Add these domains to the common production workflow, rights checks, accessibility, source provenance, owner/policy gate and correction propagation. Health, exercise, electrical safety, product standards, labor and environmental claims require domain-specific review. Industry data and regulatory requirements must be date- and jurisdiction-stamped.
